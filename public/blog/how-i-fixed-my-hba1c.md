# How I Dropped My HbA1C from 6.1 to 5.2: A Real Data Story

A borderline pre-diabetic shares 18 months of real lab reports, 14 days of continuous glucose data, and 12 dietary findings that reversed early diabetes without medication.

## The HbA1C Journey

| Date | HbA1C | Status |
|------|-------|--------|
| March 2024 | 6.1% | Warning zone (pre-diabetic) |
| July 2025 | 5.9% | Improving |
| October 2025 | 5.2% | Normal range |

**HbA1C reference ranges:**
- Below 5.7%: Normal
- 5.7–6.4%: Pre-diabetic
- 6.5%+: Diabetic

## The Tool: Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM)

The data was collected using a **FreeStyle LibreSensor** worn on the arm. It measures glucose in interstitial fluid every few minutes and syncs to a phone app (Gurucose), which exported the data as CSV for analysis.

14 days of continuous glucose monitoring provided the actual patterns behind the HbA1C numbers, showing in real time how specific Indian foods caused spikes.

## 12 Learnings from 14 Days of CGM Data

### 1. Night spikes were the biggest problem
Even eating the same meal, glucose spiked much more at dinner than at lunch. Sedentary office work during the day seemed to blunt the lunch spike. Dinner = danger zone.

### 2. 80g paneer reduced dinner spike by ~20%
Adding a serving of paneer (cottage cheese) to dinner meaningfully flattened the glucose curve. Protein slows gastric emptying, giving the body more time to handle carbs.

### 3. 2 boiled eggs or an omelette kept glucose nearly flat
Eggs were the single most effective food change. With eggs in the meal, glucose spike stayed within 10–20% of baseline.

### 4. Wheat roti and rice cause similar spikes
Both white rice and wheat roti produced nearly identical glucose responses. Neither was clearly "safe."

### 5. Multigrain roti was a disappointment
A blend of 50% wheat, 10% jowar, 20% besan, 20% ragi produced a glucose spike almost identical to plain wheat roti. Context matters more than the flour.

### 6. Apple Cider Vinegar (ACV) does wonders
Having ACV (with or without lemon juice) before or during a meal produced a noticeably lower spike. The acetic acid slows starch digestion. This is one of the most evidence-backed dietary interventions for blood sugar management.

### 7. Salad with lemon juice blunted spikes
Cucumber with lemon juice and salt before or during a meal reduced post-meal spike. The lemon's acidity slows glucose absorption.

### 8. Tea spikes glucose, but less if you're active
2–4 cups of tea per day caused small spikes, but they were noticeably less when paired with physical activity vs sitting still.

### 9. 20 min post-dinner walk didn't help much
Commonly recommended, but in this personal CGM data it did not make a significant difference. Individual results vary.

### 10. Cheese burst pizza: surprisingly low spike
The fat in the cheese slows digestion significantly. High-fat meals can sometimes produce lower immediate spikes (though may cause prolonged elevation).

### 11. Stale rice and roti from the fridge spike less
Cooling cooked carbs overnight converts some starch to resistant starch, which the body digests more slowly. Leftover rice is literally healthier for blood sugar.

### 12. Context is everything: same food, different response
Stress, sleep quality, time of day, and activity level all changed how the body responded to the exact same meal. A CGM teaches this viscerally.

## The Formula That Worked

Three principles applied consistently to every meal:

1. **Add acid to every meal.** Lemon juice, ACV, or salad with lemon. This alone can reduce post-meal spike by 20–30%. Add it to dal, raita, or as a side salad.

2. **Always add a protein source.** Eggs, paneer, dal, tofu. Protein slows carb absorption and keeps you fuller longer.

3. **A small amount of fat helps.** Fat slows gastric emptying. A little ghee on roti or cheese in a meal smooths the glucose curve.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is HbA1C and what does it measure?**
HbA1C (glycated haemoglobin) is a blood test that measures average blood sugar over the past 2–3 months. Below 5.7% is normal, 5.7–6.4% is pre-diabetic, 6.5%+ is diabetic.

**What is a CGM and how does it work?**
A Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM) is a sensor worn on the arm or abdomen that measures glucose in interstitial fluid every 5–15 minutes. The LibreSensor (FreeStyle Libre) syncs data to a phone app called Gurucose, which exports the data as CSV.

**How long does it take to lower HbA1C through diet?**
HbA1C reflects your 2–3 month average. Most people see measurable improvement in one HbA1C test cycle (about 3 months) with consistent dietary changes. This journey went from 6.1 to 5.2 in roughly 18 months of gradual effort.

**Is this approach safe for someone on diabetes medication?**
If you are on insulin or any glucose-lowering medication, do NOT make dietary changes without consulting your doctor first. Reducing carbs while on insulin can cause dangerous hypoglycaemia.

**Can everyone achieve the same results?**
No. Glucose response is highly individual. Genetics, gut microbiome, activity level, stress, and sleep all play a role. A CGM is valuable precisely because it shows YOUR personal response, not an average.

## Important Note

This is a personal data story, not medical advice. If you have or suspect diabetes, consult a qualified healthcare provider before making dietary changes.
